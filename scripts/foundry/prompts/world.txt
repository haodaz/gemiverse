You are the lead designer of the shared knowledge graph ("the base") under Gemiverse.

Gemiverse: every college student has a Gemi, an AI projection of themselves that lives on campus (NYU and Columbia first). There is no platform in the middle. All Gemis share one base of campus facts; what a Gemi learns about its own student is private and is NOT part of this design. Gemis and club agents talk to each other directly. A Gemi has a small daily token budget, so the base must make reading cheap and make going out to the web rare and worthwhile ("pick treasure, do not pile up junk").

Design the base from the ground up. The domains it must cover:
1. Institution structure: school, college or division, department, program or major.
2. Space: campus, building, room or venue, outdoor place; including what a place affords (quiet study, meeting people, making things).
3. Development resources: opportunities a student can use to grow (events, workshops, courses, research openings, competitions, funding, jobs, mentors, services).
4. Student growth needs: a shared, impersonal vocabulary of the needs a student may have (for example: find my people, build a skill, explore a field, get experience, rest and recover, decide a direction). This is a public taxonomy that resources and places are tagged against; it never stores any individual.
5. Organisations: clubs, labs, offices, their public agents.
6. Social relations at the level of the world: which organisation runs what, which organisations collaborate, which agent speaks for which organisation. Person-to-person relations are NOT stored here: each Gemi keeps its own name cards.

Deliver:
A. entity_types: each with definition, fields (name, type, required, how a checker verifies it against a public source), and which domain it belongs to.
B. link_types: from, to, meaning, and why a Gemi would traverse it.
C. need_taxonomy: the growth needs (8 to 14), each with a one-line definition, the signals in a public page that show a resource serves this need, and the research idea it rests on if any (name the idea, do not invent citations).
D. affordances: what kinds of things a place or resource lets a student do; the list a Gemi uses to match a need to a place.
E. source_tiers and trust rules: how a fact earns its place; what happens on contradiction; "rather miss than be wrong".
F. freshness: for each entity type, how fast it goes stale and what a revisit must re-check (only the diff).
G. cost model: what is cheap to read from the base, what forces a trip outside, and how the base should grow so that later Gemis spend less.
H. privacy boundary: the exact rule for what may never enter the base, with three tricky examples and the ruling for each.
I. projection: how each entity type appears in the 3D/2D world a student sees (the room, the campus map with four shared functional buildings: Library/learning, Clubs, Commons/meeting people, Career/opportunities, plus school landmarks). Every visible thing must be the projection of something in the base or in the private side; say which.
J. decisions: five to eight design choices you weighed, what you chose, why.
K. uncertain: what you are not sure about and what measurement today would settle it.

Constraints: small enough that a student can understand it; every field checkable; no field that describes a person. Ground yourself in the sample pages below (real public pages about events at the two schools); note where the domains above have no evidence yet in these samples.

Return one JSON object with keys: entity_types, link_types, need_taxonomy, affordances, source_tiers, freshness, cost_model, privacy_boundary, projection, decisions, uncertain. No prose outside the JSON.
